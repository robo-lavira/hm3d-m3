Abstract

Rapid progress in AI agents and robotics makes it a matter of the near future that humans are served by a team of robots. Such a team will receive several goals at once, each specified in whatever form is convenient for the human, such as a category name, a photo, or a spoken description. Existing benchmarks study multi-agent, multi-modal, and multi-object goal navigation in isolation, and a benchmark that places all three in one episode is missing. To fill this gap, we introduce HM3D-M³.

A team of K agents starts at distributed locations in a photorealistic indoor scene and must find an unordered set of M object goals, each given as an open-vocabulary category, an instance image, or an instance description. HM3D-M³ provides 1,000 validation episodes over 3,926 goals from 379 open-vocabulary categories, 100K training samples, and two team-level metrics, goal-level SR and makespan-normalized SPL. We further contribute two baselines. HiGA is a trained hierarchical goal assigner on top of a frozen open-vocabulary navigation policy, and LaViRA-M³ is a training-free team of VLM agents with shared mapping and shared progress. Adapted multi-robot and zero-shot methods reach 16 to 27% SR, while our baselines reach 38 to 41% with complementary strengths and orthogonal failure modes. Furthermore, we deploy LaViRA-M³ on a heterogeneous three-robot team.

Multi-Agent, Multi-Modal, and Multi-Object

Multi-agent: a team of K ∈ {2, 3, 4} agents starts at distributed, jointly reachable locations and shares a map; a goal counts as found as soon as any team member finds it. Multi-object: each episode carries M ∈ {3, 4, 5} unordered goals, all known at the start. Multi-modal: every goal is individually specified as an object category (ON), an instance-level textual description (TN), or an instance image (IIN).
Benchmark statistics: 1,000 validation episodes spanning 3,926 goals from 379 open-vocabulary categories, with balanced episode occupancy over K and M (a), balanced goal counts across the three modalities (b), goal spread that correlates with the shortest team tour (r = 0.71) (c), and diverse instance descriptions (d). 100K training samples are provided for learned methods.

Baselines

We contribute two baselines representing two paradigms: 1) HiGA, a trained hierarchical goal assigner — a Set-Transformer that predicts per-goal heat-maps and assigns goals to agents — on top of a frozen open-vocabulary navigation policy; and 2) LaViRA-M³, a training-free team of VLM agents that translate goals into Language, Vision, and Robot actions with shared top-down mapping and shared progress estimation.
HiGA: trained hierarchical goal assignment over a frozen open-vocabulary navigation policy.
LaViRA-M³: training-free team of VLM agents with shared mapping and shared progress.

Adapted multi-robot and zero-shot methods reach only 16–27% SR, while our two baselines reach 38–41% with complementary strengths: HiGA is strongest on category goals (ON), and LaViRA-M³ dominates on instance-level goals (TN / IIN) and efficiency (SPL).

Qualitative and failure analysis

Failure attribution: per-goal Sankey analysis shows the two baselines fail in orthogonal ways — LaViRA-M³ mostly loses goals at claim time (pose miss / verifier rejection), while HiGA more often never gets near the goal or fails to detect it — suggesting the two paradigms are complementary.

Real-World Deployment

We deploy LaViRA-M³ on a heterogeneous robot team: an Agilex Cobot Magic mobile manipulator and Unitree Go1 quadrupeds, streaming RGB-D observations over Wi-Fi to a local workstation that runs the VLM. In the task "find the blue robot dog in the room", the team splits up and a Go1 finds the target.
